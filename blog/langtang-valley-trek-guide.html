1. Expectation vs. Reality: The Truth About the Valley of Glaciers

First explored by legendary British mountaineer H.W. Bill Tilman in 1949, who famously wrote that "Langtang is one of the most charming valleys in the world", this narrow Himalayan canyon holds a reverent place in mountaineering lore. In ancient Tibetan Buddhist cosmology, Langtang is venerated as a "Beyul" (སྦས་ཡུལ)—a sacred hidden sanctuary consecrated by Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) in the 8th century as a place of spiritual refuge during times of global turmoil.

On April 25, 2015, a catastrophic 7.8-magnitude earthquake triggered the collapse of a massive hanging glacier on the south face of Langtang Lirung (7,227m). An estimated 40 million tons of ice, rock, and moraine obliterated Old Langtang Village. Today, passing the solemn Mani Stone Memorial Wall at the edge of the avalanche field is one of the most poignant emotional experiences in the Himalayas. Yet what trekkers witness immediately beyond is an extraordinary testament to human resilience: New Langtang Village was reconstructed 100 meters higher on the northern lateral moraine with earthquake-engineered stone masonry, Swiss-insulated teahouses, and solar arrays—transforming Langtang into one of the cleanest, warmest, and most community-focused trekking corridors in Nepal.

1. Post-Earthquake Lodges & Comfort

Expectation: Primitive conditions, fragile plywood shacks, and uncomfortable drafty dorms following the 2015 earthquake.
Reality: Langtang's teahouses are among the newest and best built in Nepal. Reconstructed with thick chiseled granite blocks, double-glazed insulated windows, plywood interior paneling, solar-heated water, and attached western toilets in many private rooms from Syabrubesi to Kyanjin Gompa.
🏔️

2. Mountain Scale & Proximity

Expectation: "Less dramatic than Everest Base Camp" because peaks top out at 7,227m rather than 8,848m.
Reality: The sheer verticality is actually more intimate than EBC. Langtang Lirung rises 3,357 meters straight up from the teahouse balconies of Kyanjin Gompa. You are encircled by a 360-degree amphitheater of hanging seracs, glacier tongues, and icy summits including Dorje Lakpa (6,966m), Gangchempo (6,387m), and Yala Peak (5,500m).
🚙

3. Kathmandu Access & Zero Flight Risk

Expectation: Stressful domestic mountain flight delays, Lukla-style weather cancellations, and lost international connections.
Reality: 100% overland accessibility. A private 4WD Mahindra Scorpio departs Kathmandu and delivers you to the trailhead in Syabrubesi in 6–7 hours. Even in foggy or rainy weather, your expedition runs on schedule without domestic airport headaches.
🥾

4. Fitness Level & Trail Terrain

Expectation: An extreme technical mountaineering trial requiring crampons, ropes, and exhausting 8-hour daily marches.
Reality: A non-technical, well-graded mountain trail along the Langtang Khola. Daily stages average 4 to 6 hours. It is the premier Himalayan journey for fit beginners, active families, and hikers with only one week of holiday time.
🧀

5. Authentic Tibetan & Tamang Culture

Expectation: Commercialized tourist shops, Westernized cafes, and losing cultural authenticity.
Reality: Deep cultural immersion. You pass historic wooden watermills grinding buckwheat, the ancient 400-year-old Kyanjin Monastery, Buddhist nunneries, prayer flag-draped chortens, and the famous 1955 Swiss-assisted Yak Cheese Factory producing artisan cheese at 3,870m.
🐾

6. Biodiversity & Red Panda Habitat

Expectation: Barren rock and scree slopes with sparse botanical life.
Reality: Langtang National Park spans five distinct bio-climatic zones—from subtropical sal and bamboo groves to subalpine birch and silver fir. It is a critical sanctuary for the endangered Red Panda (Habre), Himalayan Tahr, Musk Deer, and over 250 species of alpine birds.

The Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m) Alpine Exploration Hub

Establishing your base for two nights at Kyanjin Gompa allows you to acclimatize safely under the "Climb High, Sleep Low" physiological principle while tackling one of four breathtaking day expeditions:

Option A · Moderate

Kyanjin Ri Lower Peak (4,400m)

Duration: 2.5–3 hrs round-trip · Gain: +530m / -530m
A steady, steep switchback climb across alpine meadows directly behind Kyanjin village. Reaches a prayer-flag draped ridge overlooking the village roofs, Kyanjin Monastery, and the lower Langtang Lirung icefall. Perfect for trekkers testing their high-altitude lungs.
Option B · Gold Standard

Kyanjin Ri Main Summit (4,773m)

Duration: 4–5 hrs round-trip · Gain: +903m / -903m
Continues along the knife-edge rocky ridge past the Lower Peak to the true summit cairn. Delivers an awe-inspiring 360-degree panorama of Langtang Lirung (7,227m), Changbu (6,781m), Yansa Tsenji (6,543m), and the Tibetan border peaks. Recommended for 95% of trekkers.
Option C · High Challenge

Tserko Ri Summit (4,984m)

Duration: 7–8 hrs round-trip · Gain: +1,114m / -1,114m
Crosses the Langtang Khola eastward and tackles a grueling, steep scree and moraine climb starting at 05:00 AM. Summit offers expansive views of Shishapangma (8,027m) in Tibet, Dorje Lakpa, and the Langshisha glacier. Requires high fitness and alpine stamina.
Option D · Glacial Wilderness

Langshisha Kharka (4,100m)

Duration: 6–7 hrs round-trip · Gain: +230m / -230m
A flatter, expansive valley trek heading east along the glacial riverbed past yak pastures to the foot of Langshisha Glacier. Deep wilderness solitude beneath towering ice walls with very low altitude climbing strain.
🧀 The 1955 Swiss Yak Cheese Legacy & Sacred Mani Walls
In 1955, Swiss dairy specialist Werner Schulthess and the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) introduced traditional Swiss alpine cheese-making techniques to the Langtang Valley. Using milk from native female yaks (known locally as Naks), the Kyanjin Yak Cheese Factory continues to craft wheels of semi-hard, nutrient-dense Himalayan cheese that mature in stone cellars at 3,870m. Trekkers can tour the factory, watch the curds being pressed, and sample warm fresh curd or buy 500g blocks of aged cheese (~NPR 900) as the ultimate high-protein trail snack.
Government Tariffs & Checkpoints

2. 2026 Permits, TIMS, Checkpoints & Legal Regulations

Unlike restricted regions such as Manaslu, Upper Mustang, or Nar Phu, the Langtang Valley is an open national park corridor. This means you do not need expensive weekly Restricted Area Permits (RAP) or a minimum group requirement of two trekkers. However, entering Langtang National Park legally requires two primary government documents:

Conservation Entry

Langtang National Park Permit (LNP)

NPR 3,000 (~$23 USD) + 13% Govt VAT
  • • Enforcing Authority: Department of National Parks & Wildlife Conservation (DNPWC)
  • • SAARC Citizens: NPR 1,500 (~$11.50 USD) | Nepali Nationals: NPR 100
  • • Where Issued: Nepal Tourism Board (Kathmandu) or Dhunche Checkpoint Counter
  • • Validity: Single entry, unrestricted duration within the park boundary
  • • Documents Needed: Original passport, 2 passport photos, entry form
Safety Registration

TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System)

NPR 2,000 (~$15 USD)
  • • Enforcing Authority: Trekking Agencies Association of Nepal (TAAN) & NTB
  • • SAARC Citizens: NPR 1,000 (~$7.50 USD) | Diplomats: Exempt
  • • Where Issued: Registered government-licensed trekking agency in Kathmandu
  • • Purpose: National trekker safety registry, emergency rescue tracking & insurance verification
  • • Documents Needed: Licensed guide affiliation, emergency contact, passport scan
🛡️ 2026 Mandatory Guide Regulation (NTB & TAAN Rule)
Under official Nepal Tourism Board (NTB) regulations, all foreign individual trekkers (FITs) are legally required to hire a licensed trekking guide registered with a government-recognized agency before entering Langtang National Park. Solo trekking without an authorized guide is strictly prohibited at all army checkpoints. Your guide holds a verified NATHM (Nepal Academy of Tourism and Hotel Management) license, provides Wilderness First Responder (WFR) support, and manages all police checkpoint formalities.

5 Inviolable Legal, Cultural & Environmental Rules

1. Licensed Guide Escort

Every foreign trekker must be accompanied by an insured, licensed guide with a valid NTB registration badge.

2. Clockwise Mani Stones

Always pass Buddhist prayer walls (Mani stones) and chortens on your left (clockwise) out of sacred reverence.

3. Strict Drone Prohibition

Flying recreational drones in Langtang National Park is illegal without written permits from CAAN and DNPWC.

4. Zero High-Altitude Firewood

Deforestation is strictly penalized. Teahouse dining stoves above Ghodatabela operate strictly on dried yak dung and LPG.

5. Single-Use Plastic Bottle Ban

Pack reusable insulated bottles with purification tablets; buy safe boiled water refills from local lodge filtration stations.

3. 8-Day Gold Standard Acclimatization Itinerary

The golden rule of Himalayan high-altitude safety is: "Never increase your sleeping elevation by more than 500 meters per day above 3,000 meters." This meticulously designed 8-day itinerary establishes Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m) as a dedicated two-night base camp. By sleeping at 3,870m and climbing to 4,773m (Kyanjin Ri) or 4,984m (Tserko Ri) during daylight hours before returning to sleep, your body triggers natural erythropoietin (EPO) production and adapts smoothly with zero physiological shock.

Day 1 Kathmandu to Syabrubesi via Trishuli & Dhunche 1,460m · 130 km · 6–7 hrs Drive

Early 07:00 AM hotel pickup in Kathmandu by private 4WD Mahindra Scorpio. We leave the Kathmandu Valley via the Balaju-Kakani highway or the Galchhi highway along the Trishuli River. Pass lush terraced hills, roadside fruit stalls, and hydropower tunnels to Trishuli Bazaar and Betrawati for an authentic organic lunch. We climb steep winding mountain switchbacks up to Dhunche (1,960m)—the district capital of Rasuwa—where our guide clears the first Nepal Army checkpoint and registers our Langtang National Park permits. A scenic 45-minute descent brings us into Syabrubesi (1,460m), the bustling gateway town along the confluence of the Bhote Koshi and Langtang Khola.

Day 2 Syabrubesi to Lama Hotel via Bamboo & Langtang Khola 2,470m · 11.5 km · 5–6 hrs Trek

After a hearty breakfast, cross the long suspension bridge over the Bhote Koshi and turn east onto the stone-paved trail following the roaring Langtang Khola. We hike through a dense subtropical temperate canopy of oak, maple, alder, and towering bamboo groves. The trail ascends gently past Doman (1,680m) and reaches Bamboo (1,970m) by the riverside for a refreshing tea or lunch stop. From Bamboo, the trail steepens through moss-draped rhododendron forest, crossing a wooden bridge and ascending stone stairs past Rimche (2,400m) to reach the clearing of Lama Hotel (2,470m) nestled in the river forest.

Day 3 Lama Hotel to New Langtang Village via Ghodatabela 3,430m · 14.8 km · 6 hrs Trek

We begin climbing through hemlock, pine, and birch forest with fleeting glimpses of Langtang Lirung's white peaks between the trees. At Ghodatabela (3,030m), our guide logs our entry at the Nepal Army post. Here, the narrow river gorge opens dramatically into a classic wide, U-shaped glacial valley. We pass Buddhist water-driven prayer wheels, wooden yak pastures, and stone shelters at Thangshyap (3,140m) and Chamki. We cross the 2015 avalanche debris field, pausing in solemn silence at the Memorial Chorten wall, before arriving at New Langtang Village (3,430m), thriving with modern stone-masonry lodges.

Day 4 Langtang Village to Kyanjin Gompa & Swiss Cheese Factory 3,870m · 6.8 km · 3–4 hrs Trek

We pass Mundu (3,550m), Sindum, and the valley's longest carved Mani stone wall, always walking on the left side. Crossing wooden footbridges over braided glacial streams beneath the gargantuan hanging icefall of Langtang Lirung, we enter the amphitheater basin of Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m) by lunchtime. In the afternoon, visit the 400-year-old Kyanjin Monastery, tour the historic Swiss-backed Yak Cheese Factory, and enjoy fresh apple pie at the local high-altitude bakeries.

(Trekkers opting for Tserko Ri start at 05:00 AM for the 7-8 hour high-altitude challenge to 4,984m). Descend safely to Kyanjin Gompa for a hot lunch and a restful afternoon.

5. Kyanjin Ri (4,773m) Summit Anatomy & Altitude Safety

While sleeping elevations in Langtang top out comfortably at 3,870m in Kyanjin Gompa—well below the extreme danger zones of Thorong High Camp (4,880m) or Gorak Shep (5,164m)—ascending Kyanjin Ri (4,773m) or Tserko Ri (4,984m) exposes your body to atmospheric pressure where effective oxygen drops to 56% of sea level. Understanding the physiology of hypoxic adaptation is critical to a safe, successful summit.

Stage 1 · 05:30 AM

The North Ridge Switchbacks (3,870m → 4,400m)

Depart Kyanjin Gompa at first light. Climb steady, steep zigzags across tussock grass, dwarf juniper, and rhododendron scrub (+530m gain). Reach the Lower Viewpoint marked by colorful prayer flags in ~2 hours with a breathtaking view of Kyanjin Monastery below.
Stage 2 · 07:30 AM

The Knife-Edge Summit Crest (4,400m → 4,773m)

Follow the narrowing rocky spine over loose scree and shattered granite blocks (+373m gain). A 1.5-hour push along the crest leads to the main summit cairn of Kyanjin Ri (4,773m) facing the sheer 3,400m south face of Langtang Lirung.
Alternative · 05:00 AM

Tserko Ri High Expedition (4,984m)

A grueling 7–8 hour alpine push crossing the Langtang Khola eastward to Yala pasture (+1,114m gain). Ascends frozen lateral moraines and boulder fields. Delivers expansive views of Shishapangma (8,027m) in Tibet and Dorje Lakpa.
Protocol · 11:30 AM

Descent & "Climb High, Sleep Low"

Descend carefully using trekking poles back to Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m) by late morning. Dropping 900 meters back to your lodge sleeping altitude stimulates erythropoietin (EPO) production while eliminating prolonged hypoxic stress.

Altitude Illness Clinical Pathology & Emergency SOP

Every Best Treks Nepal expedition leader is certified in Wilderness First Aid (WFA) and carries pulse oximeters to assess blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) twice daily:

Pathology Diagnostic Symptoms & SpO2 Standard Field Test Pharmacological & Evacuation SOP
Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) Throbbing frontal headache, nausea, fatigue, insomnia, loss of appetite. SpO2: 75%–84%. Lake Louise AMS Score ≥ 3 with headache present Halt ascent immediately. Rest 24–48h at same altitude. Hydrate 4L daily. Ibuprofen (400–600mg) for headache; Acetazolamide / Diamox (125mg–250mg BID) to accelerate acclimatization.
High Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE) Severe ataxia (loss of physical coordination), confusion, slurred speech, hallucinations, irrational behavior. SpO2 < 65%. Administer Dexamethasone (8mg stat, then 4mg every 6 hrs), portable hyperbaric Gamow bag, emergency satellite helicopter SOS.
High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE) Severe breathlessness at rest, persistent wet cough, gurgling in lungs, pink frothy sputum, cyanosis (blue lips). SpO2 < 60%. Audible chest crackles/rales with ear or stethoscope CRITICAL MEDICAL EMERGENCY. Immediate descent. Nifedipine (20mg sustained release), high-flow supplemental oxygen (4–6 L/min), emergency helicopter evacuation from Kyanjin Gompa helipad.

Insider Local Knowledge

6. "What No One Tells You" — 10 Uncensored Langtang Secrets

Unfiltered field intelligence and cultural realities from our native Tamang and Sherpa guides that mainstream commercial travel brochures overlook:

01 Lodge Quality

New Langtang Lodges Outperform Traditional EBC Teahouses

Because the entire settlement of Langtang Village was rebuilt following the 2015 earthquake, the teahouses feature modern stone architecture, high ceilings, double-glazed insulated windows, solar arrays, and more attached western bathrooms than legacy huts on older Himalayan routes.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Choose newly built stone lodges in New Langtang and Kyanjin Gompa for significantly warmer rooms and reliable solar hot water.
02 Overland Transit

The Highway Drive is Bumpier Than the Actual Trek

The 130 km road from Kathmandu to Syabrubesi along the Pasang Lhamu Highway includes roughly 35 km of unpaved, winding mountain switchbacks between Betrawati and Dhunche. Public buses take 9–10 agonizing hours, while private 4WD Mahindra Scorpios complete the trip in 6–7 hours with cushioned suspensions.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Never take a crowded public bus if you are prone to motion sickness. Private 4WD jeep transport is essential for physical comfort.
03 Altitude Sickness

The Lama Hotel to Langtang Elevation Jump Catches Trekkers Off-Guard

Day 3 ascends from Lama Hotel (2,470m) to New Langtang (3,430m)—a net gain of +960 meters in a single day, crossing the critical 3,000m threshold. Trekkers who rush this stage often experience mild frontal headaches by evening.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Take a relaxed 1-hour lunch break at Ghodatabela (3,030m) to rest your cardiovascular system, and consume 4 liters of warm fluids daily.
04 Electronics

Sub-Zero Kyanjin Nights Drain Phone & Camera Batteries in Hours

Night temperatures in Kyanjin Gompa drop between -5°C and -12°C. Lithium-ion batteries left out on bedside tables will drop from 100% to dead by morning. Lodges charge NPR 250–300 per device recharge via solar batteries.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Always store your smartphone, camera batteries, and 20,000mAh power bank inside your sleeping bag near your feet overnight.
05 Gastronomy

Artisanal Swiss Yak Cheese is Made Fresh Daily at 3,870m

Since 1955, the Kyanjin Yak Cheese Factory has produced pure, organic semi-hard cheese from female yaks (Naks). Local teahouses bake fresh yak cheese pizzas, pastas, and warm apple pastries that rival Thamel bakeries.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Purchase a 500g vacuum-sealed block of aged yak cheese at the factory for ~NPR 900 ($7 USD) as a rich protein snack for your summit push.
06 Summit Tactics

Tserko Ri is Significantly More Strenuous Than Kyanjin Ri

Online itineraries often present Kyanjin Ri (4,773m, 4 hrs) and Tserko Ri (4,984m, 8 hrs) as interchangeable. In reality, Tserko Ri involves +1,114m of vertical climbing over icy boulder moraines and steep loose scree, requiring advanced alpine endurance.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Summit Kyanjin Ri first. Only attempt Tserko Ri if you feel energetic, experienced, and completely symptom-free above 4,400m.
07 Indigenous Culture

Langtang Lineage is Distinct from Everest Sherpas

The indigenous inhabitants of the upper valley identify as Tamang and Tibetan Hyolmo. Their dialect, distinctive hand-woven wool aprons (Pangden), prayer ceremonies, and ancient Bon-animist roots differ significantly from Solukhumbu Sherpa traditions.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Greet locals with "Tashi Delek" (Tibetan) or "Lhaso Fulla" (Tamang) and always walk clockwise around Mani walls and chortens.
08 Wildlife Safety

Yak Caravans Have Total Right-of-Way on Suspension Bridges

Caravans of heavily loaded yaks and dzos (yak-cow hybrids) move quickly along narrow cliffside ledges and suspension bridges. Standing on the outer cliff edge when animals pass creates a severe risk of being pushed into the river gorge.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Whenever bells announce an approaching herd, immediately step off the trail onto the mountain/wall side and wait until all animals pass.
09 Joint Protection

Trekking Poles are Crucial for the 1,400m Descent on Day 6

Descending from Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m) down to Lama Hotel (2,470m) involves dropping 1,400 meters vertically over 21.6 km of continuous stone steps. Without trekking poles, this puts immense shock loading onto knee joints and quadriceps.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Use two shock-absorbing trekking poles adjusted 5–10 cm longer for downhill walking to absorb up to 25% of body impact forces.
10 Eco Protection

Plastic Water Bottles are Banned Above Ghodatabela

To protect Langtang National Park from plastic pollution, single-use plastic water bottles are prohibited in upper villages. Commercial bottled water costs upwards of NPR 300 in Kyanjin Gompa and cannot be recycled locally.

💡 Sherpa Advice: Bring two 1-liter wide-mouth Nalgene bottles, fill them with boiled water at lodges, and treat trail stream water with Aquatabs or a SteriPEN.

❓ Comprehensive Expedition FAQ & Legal Guidance

9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Expert-verified answers to the 10 most critical questions regarding permits, fitness, costs, and trail logistics in the Langtang Valley:

1. How difficult is the Langtang Valley Trek compared to Annapurna or Everest?
The Langtang Valley Trek is rated Moderate (Grade 3 out of 5). It is physically gentler and shorter (65–77 km over 7–8 days) than Everest Base Camp (130 km) or the Annapurna Circuit (160+ km). Highest sleeping elevation is Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m). Any healthy person with basic walking fitness who prepares with 4–6 weeks of stair-climbing or jogging can succeed comfortably.
2. What permits are legally mandatory in 2026, and how much do they cost?
You need two (2) permits:
  • Langtang National Park Entry Permit (LNP): NPR 3,000 (~$23 USD) + 13% VAT for foreigners (NPR 1,500 for SAARC).
  • TIMS Card: NPR 2,000 (~$15 USD) per person (NPR 1,000 for SAARC).
Langtang is NOT a Restricted Area, so no expensive weekly Restricted Area Permit (RAP) is required.
3. Is a licensed guide mandatory for Langtang Valley in 2026?
Yes. Under Nepal Tourism Board rules enforced at park checkpoints (Dhunche, Syabrubesi, Ghodatabela), all foreign trekkers must be accompanied by a certified, licensed guide registered with an authorized agency like Best Treks Nepal.
4. How do I get to Syabrubesi from Kathmandu?
The 130 km journey is 100% overland. We recommend a private 4WD Mahindra Scorpio jeep (6–7 hours, ~$140–$165 per vehicle split among your group), which offers comfortable suspensions and flexible photo stops. Public buses operate daily from Machhapokhari in Kathmandu ($7–$10, 8–10 hours) but are crowded and slow.
5. Which peak is better: Kyanjin Ri (4,773m) or Tserko Ri (4,984m)?
Kyanjin Ri (4,773m) is the gold standard for most trekkers (3.5–4 hours round-trip, +900m gain) with spectacular 360-degree views of Langtang Lirung right above the village. Tserko Ri (4,984m) is an intense 7–9 hour high-alpine mission (+1,114m gain) offering expansive views into Tibet, ideal for experienced, strong hikers.
6. Are there ATMs or credit card facilities in the valley?
No. There are zero ATMs or digital credit card terminals beyond Dhunche. Trekkers must withdraw all cash in Kathmandu in Nepalese Rupees (NPR 25,000–35,000 for package clients; NPR 45,000–55,000 for DIY).
7. What are the electricity, charging, and Wi-Fi conditions?
Hydro-electricity and rooftop solar power are available in all major villages. Phone charging costs NPR 200–300 per full charge. Wi-Fi (AirJaldi / local networks) is available up to Kyanjin Gompa for NPR 300–500 per day. NTC mobile network provides 4G data coverage up to Ghodatabela and intermittent 3G in Kyanjin.
8. What food is available, and is the famous yak cheese safe to eat?
Teahouses serve Dal Bhat (unlimited rice and lentil soup), porridge, Tibetan bread, pancake stacks, vegetable fried noodles, potato stews, and momos. The artisanal Swiss-method yak cheese in Kyanjin Gompa is pasteurized, extremely safe, delicious, and rich in protein!
9. Can I combine Langtang Valley with Gosaikunda Lakes or Tamang Heritage?
Yes! You can easily extend your trek by branching at Rimche towards the sacred alpine lakes of Gosaikunda (4,380m) across the Laurebina Pass (4,610m) to Helambu (adding 5–6 days), or starting with the cultural Tamang Heritage Trail (adding 4 days).
10. What is the emergency medical and helicopter evacuation protocol?
Best Treks Nepal guides carry pulse oximeters and medical kits. In critical emergencies or severe AMS/HAPE, your guide activates satellite emergency SOS for 24/7 helicopter evacuation to Kathmandu hospitals. Ensure your travel insurance covers emergency helicopter rescue up to 5,000 meters.
