Geographic Anatomy & ACAP Conservation

Nepal's Most Diverse Himalayan Wilderness

The Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) is Nepal's largest protected area, encompassing 7,629 km² across the Gandaki Province. Established in 1986, it contains a staggering biodiversity gradient — from subtropical lowlands at 790m to the summit of Annapurna I at 8,091m — encompassing eight of Nepal's ecological zones in a single trek.

The region spans three distinct trekking corridors:

1

The Circuit Corridor (1,000m – 5,416m)

The classic Annapurna Circuit circumnavigates the entire massif via Manang, Thorong La (5,416m), and Mustang, descending through Jomsom's wind-sculpted high desert valleys.

2

The Sanctuary Bowl (1,070m – 4,130m)

Annapurna Base Camp (4,130m) sits within a dramatic glacial cirque enclosed by 7,000m+ peaks on all sides — one of the world's most spectacular mountain amphitheaters.

3

The Poon Hill Panorama Zone (1,070m – 3,210m)

The Ghorepani-Poon Hill circuit offers Nepal's most rewarding short trek with sunrise panoramas over 8 peaks above 7,000m including Dhaulagiri and the Annapurna south face.

Mandatory Annapurna Permits & Fees 2026

All trekkers must carry valid ACAP and TIMS permits when entering the conservation area. We handle all permit procurement on your behalf.

ACAP Permit

NPR 3,000

Approx. USD $23 per person. Required for all trekkers entering the Annapurna Conservation Area. Valid for the entire duration of your trek.

TIMS Card

USD $20

Trekkers' Information Management System card. Mandatory for all trekkers in Nepal. Individual trekkers pay USD $20; group trekkers pay USD $10.

Nar-Phu Restricted Area

USD $90/week

Special Restricted Area Permit (RAP) required for Nar Phu Valley. First week USD $90/person, each additional week USD $45/person. Requires licensed guide.

Best Treks Nepal handles all permit procurement — ACAP, TIMS, and Restricted Area Permits — as part of our all-inclusive trek packages. No queuing at Kathmandu offices required.

The Annapurna Circuit crosses Thorong La (5,416m) — one of the highest trekking passes in the world. Proper acclimatization is essential.

AMS Warning Signs

Severe headache, vomiting, loss of coordination, or confusion — DESCEND IMMEDIATELY. Never sleep at higher elevation if symptoms persist.

Annapurna Seasonal Climate & Best Windows

Unlike Everest, the Annapurna region has remarkably consistent Spring and Autumn windows, with the rain-shadow effect allowing trekking in Mustang even during monsoon.

Season Months Temp at Pass Conditions Rating
Spring Mar – May -5°C to +10°C Clear mornings, afternoon clouds. Rhododendron bloom. Crowded. Excellent
Monsoon Jun – Aug +5°C to +15°C Heavy rain on south slopes. Mustang/Circuit rain-shadow zones viable. Selective
Autumn Sep – Nov -8°C to +8°C Crystal clear skies, sharpest visibility. Peak season — book 3+ months early. Excellent
Winter Dec – Feb -20°C to -5°C Thorong La may be closed. Poon Hill and ABC feasible for experienced trekkers. Challenging
Gurung, Thakali & Manang Heritage

Living Cultures of the Annapurna Corridor

The Annapurna region is the homeland of the proud Gurung people — formerly world-renowned Gorkha soldiers — who have farmed the lower terraced slopes for centuries. Higher up, the Thakali community of Mustang and Jomsom are legendary for their hospitality and spiced cuisine. In the high valleys approaching Thorong La, the Manang people maintain a semi-nomadic trading culture with deep Tibetan Buddhist roots.

Muktinath Temple (3,800m) — Sacred to both Hindus and Buddhists. 108 water spouts symbolize purification.
Ghandruk Village — A living museum of Gurung culture, dance, and architecture at 1,940m.
Braga Monastery — A 900-year-old Buddhist gompa overlooking the high Manang valley.

Cultural Etiquette Essentials

Clockwise around mani walls — Always walk clockwise around prayer walls, chortens, and monasteries.
Remove shoes at gompas — Always remove footwear before entering monastery prayer halls.
"Namaste" greeting — Press palms together with a slight bow as a respectful greeting to locals.
No plastic in ACAP — Plastic bottles banned inside the conservation area. Carry a reusable water bottle and iodine tablets.

Teahouses, Lodges & Trail Cuisine

The Annapurna region has Nepal's most developed teahouse network — from basic guesthouses to comfortable lodges with hot showers and WiFi.

Standard Teahouses

Basic twin/double rooms with communal bathrooms. Cold water wash (hot shower extra ~$2). Perfect for budget trekkers. Blankets provided. Average: $8-15/night.

Thakali Cuisine

The Thakali "dal bhat" of the Annapurna is famous across Nepal — fluffy rice, thick lentil soup, seasonal vegetables, and achaar (pickle). Includes unlimited refills. Best value fuel on the trail.

Connectivity

WiFi available in most lodges up to Manang (3,519m) and Jomsom (2,720m) on the Mustang side. Higher on the circuit, Ncell sim cards offer 4G through Thorong Phedi (4,541m) on clear days.

Annapurna Packing & Gear Checklist

What you pack determines your comfort on Thorong La. Layering is essential for the massive elevation range (800m to 5,416m) encountered on the Circuit.

Clothing Layers

  • Moisture-wicking base layers (top + bottom)
  • Insulated down jacket (700-fill minimum)
  • Waterproof hardshell jacket + trousers
  • Fleece mid-layer pullover
  • Thermal balaclava + fleece gloves
  • Warm trekking hat + sun hat for lower valleys

Footwear & Hardware

  • Waterproof ankle-height trekking boots (broken in)
  • Trekking gaiters for snow sections near Thorong La
  • Adjustable trekking poles (carbon)
  • 40L daypack + 60-70L porter bag
  • UV400 glacier sunglasses + SPF 50+ sunscreen
  • Headlamp (lithium batteries) + power bank
Porter Weight Limit: Maximum 20kg porter load per porter (Nepal Government regulation). We include 1 porter per 2 trekkers in our groups.

Frequently Asked Questions: Annapurna Region

Everything you need to know about ACAP permits, Thorong La crossing, seasonal windows, teahouses, and booking your Annapurna expedition.

Planning

What is the best season for the Annapurna Circuit trek?

The two peak seasons are Spring (March–May) and Autumn (September–November). Spring brings rhododendron blooms and stable conditions before afternoon clouds build. Autumn offers the clearest mountain views with post-monsoon freshness. October is the absolute peak month — book 4-6 months in advance for autumn departures.

Permits

What permits do I need for the Annapurna Circuit?

You need two mandatory permits: (1) ACAP (Annapurna Conservation Area Permit) — NPR 3,000 (~$23 USD), and (2) TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System) — $20 USD for independent trekkers. For the Nar-Phu Valley extension, a Restricted Area Permit is required ($90 USD/week). Best Treks Nepal handles all permits as part of our package price.

Altitude Safety

How difficult is the Thorong La Pass crossing?

The Thorong La Pass (5,416m) is a long, demanding day — typically 6-8 hours of ascent (700m vertical) followed by a 1,600m descent to Muktinath. Always start by 4-5 AM to avoid dangerous afternoon winds. After a proper rest day in Manang (mandatory), the crossing is achievable for most fit trekkers. AMS (Altitude Mountain Sickness) is the main risk — our guides carry supplemental oxygen and Diamox.

Monsoon Season

Is it possible to trek Annapurna during monsoon?

Yes — uniquely, the Annapurna region's rain-shadow zones (Mustang and Manang-Thorong corridor) receive very little monsoon rainfall. Upper Mustang from Jomsom northward remains dry and trekable June–August. The southern approach routes (Ghorepani, Modi Khola Valley for ABC) experience heavy monsoon rain and are best avoided during this period.

Lodges & Food

What food and accommodation is available on the circuit?

The Annapurna Circuit has Nepal's most developed teahouse network. Accommodation ranges from basic guesthouses ($5-15/night) to comfortable lodges with en-suite bathrooms in Besisahar and Pokhara. The famous Thakali dal bhat (unlimited refills) is available in Jomsom and Kagbeni — widely considered Nepal's finest trail food. Most teahouses also offer pasta, pizza, momos, and apple products (famous in Marpha village).

Insurance

Do I need travel insurance for the Annapurna trek?

Yes — travel insurance is mandatory for all our trekkers. It must include helicopter evacuation coverage to minimum USD $50,000 and medical coverage up to the trek's maximum altitude (5,416m for the Circuit). World Nomads, True Traveller, and Battleface are popular options for high-altitude trekking in Nepal. We verify your policy at check-in.

Advance Booking

How far in advance should I book for autumn season?

For October–November departures (peak season), we recommend booking 4-6 months in advance. Good teahouses in strategic acclimatization villages like Manang, Thorong Phedi, and Muktinath fill very quickly. Our groups are capped at 10 trekkers maximum to ensure personalized service and teahouse reservation flexibility.
